Does FRAP's competition-graph structure even make sense for an always-on movement?

You hit this directly with — a movement that's never gated by phase choice doesn't fit FRAP's "mutually competing phase pairs" assumption at all.
That's a structural question, not just an implementation detail: is forcing always-on movements into the same embedding space as competing ones actually doing anything useful, or is it dead weight the network learns to ignore?
