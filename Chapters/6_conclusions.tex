\vspace*{100pt}

\noindent \lettrine{T}{his thesis} has developed and evaluated methods for \gls{rltsc} in realistic multimodal scenarios, with a particular focus on the integration of pedestrian and cyclist traffic. In this way, we have addressed the stated problem and provided answers to the research questions posed at its start.

\medskip

\noindent After the introduction, the beginning of this work laid out some definitions and presented \gls{tsc} as a \gls{rl} problem, to then offer a comprehensive review of the state of the art of multimodal \gls{tsc} and \gls{rltsc}, highlighting the limitations of existing approaches in handling multimodal traffic and the need for more realistic evaluation scenarios.

Then, the methods followed were described, regarding the simulation environment and the proposed \gls{rltsc} methods developed, along with their baselines.

The fourth chapter explained how the evaluation of the methods was carried out, including the definition of realistic scenarios, the experimental setup, the metrics used, and the results obtained.

Finally, the fifth chapter discussed these results and their implications, while also acknowledging the limitations of this work and suggesting future research directions.

\medskip 

\noindent We can summarize the main contributions of this work as 

\begin{enumerate}[I.]
   \item We have developed a number of realistic scenarios based in the city of Copenhagen, with multimodal traffic demand reflecting real data, accurate and complex intersection layouts and a variety of infrastructure elements. 
   \item We have adapted and extended, as baselines, existing reinforcement learning methods for traffic signal control to include cyclist traffic.
   \item We have proposed two reinforcement learning methods for traffic signal control that consider both pedestrians and cyclists, using existing methods as a starting point.
   \item We have performed several experiments in the developed scenarios to evaluate the performance of the proposed methods and the extended baselines.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent In conclusion, this thesis has strived to make a useful contribution to the field of multimodal \gls{rltsc}. We hope that the findings of this work can inform future research and development in the field, contributing to the improvement of urban mobility and the consideration of all road users.

\medskip 

\noindent For, as was stated in the introduction, the ultimate goal of traffic engineering should be to move persons, regardless of their mode of transport.
\medskip 

\noindent Traffic signals may control traffic; they should do so by serving \textit{people}.
