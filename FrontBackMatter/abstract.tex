\noindent{\LARGE Abstract}

\vspace{1cm}

Reinforcement learning can provide robust algorithms for traffic signal control.
 However, these approaches usually focus exclusively on automobiles, not
 accounting for other road users like pedestrians and cyclists. This limitation
 undermines their applicability to real scenarios and neglects the relevance of
 active mobility modes in transportation systems. This thesis aims to extend the
 reinforcement learning framework developed by Rodrigues and Lima Azevedo (2019)
 to include pedestrian and cyclist agents.
The thesis will design realistic multimodal traffic scenarios in SUMO, adapt RL
 representations to incorporate pedestrians and cyclists, and redefine the
 reward function to consider all users. The expected outcome is the development
 of a more realistic framework that enables a greater understanding of urban
 mobility.
