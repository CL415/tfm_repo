\noindent{\LARGE Acknowledgements}

\vspace{1cm}

Civil engineering is never a solitary art. No matter how ambitious its
realizations can be —maybe a majestic bridge spanning across the sea, maybe a
humble bus stop in a tiny village—, the works of civil engineering are not the 
feat of a single virtuoso, but of many people with very diverse backgrounds.
From the labourers on site to the clerks in the offices: all projects are a collective endeavour, and thus their credit is manifold.

While this Master thesis could seem far away from those projects, dealing with algorithms and simulations instead of loads and materials, it is a civil engineering project nevertheless, and as such its realization would never have been possible by myself alone. I am, hence, very grateful to the many people who have contributed to this thesis in one way or the other.


I would like to start by acknowledging the great work of the many academics that have studied the problem of traffic signal control before me, and whose findings have provided a starting point in which to start my research. I am certainly in debt, too, to the researchers and developers who have shared with the world their knowledge, experiments, and software. This thesis is founded in 
open source: the SUMO traffic simulator, and the RESCO and T-REX frameworks are the selfless work of many talented people; I cannot go on without thanking them and the open source community in general.
